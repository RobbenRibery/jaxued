Editor-Transfer PLR: Three-Seed Diagnostic

Methodical diagnosis of the latest completed seed 0, 1, and 2 editor-transfer PLR runs.

Aug 21, 2026, 11:53 AM UTC

Technical summary

The estimator is a sensible measure of one-step, learner-conditioned local transfer, but these three runs do not establish it as a reliable or effective PLR objective. Smoothed mean gain fell from 0.001472 to 0.000315 (-78.6%). Fresh-source virtual transfer almost disappeared: 0.000860 → 0.000015.

This is not only target saturation. Pre-update target return rose from 0.674 to 0.834, but headroom-normalized gain still fell -41.7% overall and -87.9% for new levels.

A weak ranking tail remains, but it is not proven stable enough for the configured sampler. Late in training, 20.5% of source scores were positive by more than one target-bank SE and only 1.8% exceeded two. No repeated-rescore or top-k stability measurement exists.

Policy performance improved, then regressed and varied sharply by seed. Smoothed mean solve rate peaked at 24.1% and ended at 20.5%. With no matched control arm, that path cannot be credited to transfer scoring.

Fresh-level transfer collapses; replay retains only a weak residual

The mixed aggregate is roughly 80% replay / 20% new. In the final smoothed window, replay gain was 0.000388, while new-level gain was 0.000015. Seed 1's final new-level mean was negative (-0.000048).

That distinction is substantive, not cosmetic. New scores come from a discarded virtual PPO update and control admission to the PLR bank. Replay scores follow a persistent update. By the end, the method still detects small local gains on already-selected replay levels, but it has nearly stopped finding fresh sources whose virtual update transfers to their edited neighborhoods.

Relative separation survives, but the top PLR decision is fragile

The smoothed 90th-percentile score fell -69.5%, while target-bank SE fell -70.4%. Because both shrink together, relative score/SE diagnostics degrade less than raw gain.

The late top-two gap averaged 0.003347, only 0.57× mean per-source SE. This is a heuristic comparison, not a test: edited targets are correlated, and the SE omits source-rollout, PPO-update, and repeated-evaluation noise.

The risk is amplified by rank temperature 0.3. With 4,000 levels, the score component places 87.2% of its mass on rank 1 and 98.9% on the top four. Because score priority contributes 70% of the final replay weights, it contributes 61.0 percentage points of total sampling probability to rank 1 before staleness is added. Top-rank reproducibility is therefore the decisive missing diagnostic.

The PLR bank is structurally healthy, but its stored score range compresses

The bank reached all 4,000 valid entries by the middle phase, and the attached-target count matched the valid bank size. Its stored 90th-percentile score fell from 0.011375 to 0.004307; the median remained slightly negative.

PLR therefore still has a positive tail to prioritize, but these logs do not show whether the tail's identity is reproducible. Stored entries were evaluated at different learner checkpoints, so the bank is also a mixture of current and stale score estimates.

Learning continues after score shrinkage, then regresses unevenly

Mean solve rate rose from 4.2% in the first smoothed window to 24.1%, then fell -3.6 percentage points. Mean evaluation return fell 19.7% from its peak.

  • Seed 0 (`k3t9umt7`): 19.0% final solve; 39.6% peak at 23.0k updates; -20.6 pp peak-to-final; final overall/new/replay gain 0.000278 / 0.000062 / 0.000331.
  • Seed 1 (`7vdx47w2`): 17.8% final solve; 22.5% peak at 24.4k updates; -4.7 pp peak-to-final; final overall/new/replay gain 0.000319 / -0.000048 / 0.000409.
  • Seed 2 (`v9bj04bs`): 24.6% final solve; 26.3% peak at 17.4k updates; -1.6 pp peak-to-final; final overall/new/replay gain 0.000349 / 0.000029 / 0.000424.

Policy entropy fell from 0.285 to 0.018. Low entropy plus highly concentrated replay is a plausible specialization or forgetting mechanism, especially for seed 0, but the association is descriptive rather than causal.

Final generalization is narrow rather than uniform

Three of eight fixed evaluation levels—StandardMaze, Labyrinth2, and LabyrinthFlipped—remained at 0% in the final smoothed window for every seed. SixteenRooms was strongest; StandardMaze3 varied sharply by seed.

  • SixteenRooms: 71.3% mean solve, 7.1% seed SD, 65.0–79.0% seed range, 0.509 mean return.
  • Labyrinth: 41.3% mean solve, 5.9% seed SD, 37.0–48.0% seed range, 0.145 mean return.
  • SixteenRooms2: 35.0% mean solve, 13.5% seed SD, 22.0–49.0% seed range, 0.112 mean return.
  • StandardMaze3: 15.0% mean solve, 17.8% seed SD, 1.0–35.0% seed range, 0.061 mean return.
  • StandardMaze2: 1.0% mean solve, 1.7% seed SD, 0.0–3.0% seed range, 0.003 mean return.
  • Labyrinth2, LabyrinthFlipped, and StandardMaze: 0.0% for every seed, with 0.000 mean return.

Transfer evaluation dominates the budget, and cumulative telemetry overflowed

Each update evaluates 32 × 128 targets both before and after the source PPO update for up to 250 steps. Across 30,000 updates, that is 61.44B transfer-evaluation steps versus 245.76M source-rollout steps: a 250× multiplier. Transfer evaluation is 99.60% of reconstructed interactions. Mean runtime was 79.0 minutes per seed.

The interval counter is consistent at 409.6M steps every 200 updates. However, cumulative num_env_steps, transfer/eval_env_steps_total, and derived sps overflow signed int32 14 times per run. Those fields are invalid for sample-efficiency or throughput comparisons here; reconstructed totals use the non-overflowing interval counter.

Scope and metric definitions

The cohort is exactly the latest completed runs for seeds 0, 1, and 2: k3t9umt7, 7vdx47w2, and v9bj04bs. All share 30,000 updates, 32 source environments, 128 fixed targets per source, 16 mutation edits per chain, Robust PLR, ACCEL off, exploratory gradients off, rank prioritization, temperature 0.3, and staleness coefficient 0.3.

For source slot i and target b, target gain is post-update return minus pre-update return. The source score is its mean over 128 targets. Target-bank SE is the sample SD over those gains divided by sqrt(128). Score/SE values are diagnostics, not formal hypothesis-test statistics.

"New" is a freshly generated source scored through a discarded virtual PPO update. "Replay" is a bank source trained by the persistent PPO update. Each W&B point averages qualifying updates within one 200-update interval.

Analysis method

  • Verified finish state, seeds, group, configuration equality, 150-row coverage, no missing requested transfer fields, and 200-update spacing.
  • Compared per-seed means in early (0–5k), middle (12.5–17.5k), and late (25–30k) phases.
  • Used trailing 10-log windows for endpoints and peaks to reduce noise from 10 evaluation attempts per level.
  • Reconstructed interaction cost from the interval transfer-step counter and static source-rollout configuration.
  • Treated score/performance relationships as descriptive; no timing correlation was interpreted causally.

Limitations and unresolved validity questions

  1. No causal baseline: no matched MaxMC, random-ranking, uncertainty-adjusted, or no-transfer arm.
  2. No rank-stability test: the same source batch was not repeatedly rescored, so top-1/top-4 reproducibility is unknown.
  3. Source-conditioned target distributions: each source is ranked on its own edited neighborhood, not a common target set. Raw score mixes source-update quality with neighborhood difficulty and sensitivity.
  4. Incomplete uncertainty: target-bank SE excludes source-rollout noise, PPO noise, repeated action-sampling noise, target correlation, duplicate targets, and score staleness.
  5. Saturation: target pre-return increases, although headroom normalization shows this does not fully explain the decline.
  6. Coarse evaluation: 10 attempts per level produce quantized solve rates; smoothing reduces but does not remove that uncertainty.

Recommended next steps

  1. Fix int32 cumulative telemetry before any baseline comparison.
  2. Validate ranking stability before changing the score formula: repeatedly rescore identical source/bank pairs and measure top-1/top-4 overlap and rank correlation.
  3. Separate admission from replay: test transfer score as replay priority while using a competence- or novelty-aware admission criterion for new levels.
  4. Use two matched budgets: equal training updates and equal reconstructed interactions or wall-clock. The 250× evaluation multiplier makes update-only comparison insufficient.
  5. Run a frozen common-audit experiment: from cloned learner states, compare high-transfer, middle, and random source choices with identical PPO budgets.
  6. Do not simply enlarge the same target bank: that reduces SE but does not increase expected gain. If saturation matters, test calibrated mutation radii or competence-frontier targets while keeping the audit distribution frozen.

Further questions

  • Is the same source still top-ranked when its fixed bank is reevaluated with independent rollout randomness?
  • Does transfer scoring remain useful if restricted to replay rather than bank admission?
  • Is seed 0's regression associated with repeated sampling of a narrow top-ranked subset?
  • Does a common-target or headroom-normalized score predict held-out improvement better than source-conditioned raw gain?
  • At equal true interaction or wall-clock cost, does this scorer outperform Robust MaxMC?
